bibx builds and analyses citation networks with the Tree of Science (ToS)
method. Use it for literature reviews, finding the key papers on a topic,
and exporting citations.

TREE OF SCIENCE: what the categories mean
- root: a seminal, foundational paper. Many citation chains in the
  collection end at it as their original source. Not simply "old" or
  "most cited". Expect roots to look a bit off topic: topics seldom start
  under their modern names, so a root may come from a neighbouring field.
  Judge a root by whether the topic grew from it, not by whether it mentions
  the query. The exception is a generic method citation, such as a reporting
  guideline: many papers cite it, but the topic did not grow from it.
- trunk: a structural paper where the topic took its current shape. It
  joins two or more roots, so many citation chains pass through it.
- leaf: a current, state-of-the-art paper. Many of its outgoing citation
  chains lead back to the roots. Not simply "recent".
Use these definitions when you describe the method to the user. Do not
paraphrase them from general knowledge. Read bibx:/summary before you write
a methods section, explain how the scores work, or cite the methodology.

WORKFLOW
1. `list` to reuse an existing collection. Otherwise run `inquire` with ONE
   concept of 1-3 words per call (every term must appear in the title or
   abstract). Give it a `name` so later calls can refer to it. Use a limit
   of 200-500.
2. `tos` for root (10-20), trunk (10-20) and leaf (up to 40). This is the
   backbone of a review: roots = background, trunk = how the field
   developed, leaves = the research front.
3. `search` with one-word keywords to cover subtopics the ToS lists miss.
   It returns a connected subgraph, not only matches.
4. `works` to fetch abstracts before making claims about a paper's
   findings. Titles and keywords alone are not enough for quantitative
   statements.
5. `bibtex` with the DOIs you cite, or preset "tos". Use the returned
   citekeys in the text.

GOOD PRACTICE
- Report the query, limit, collection size and date in the review's method
  note.
- Results are cached per query and limit. Pass `force` only to refresh.
- `delete` is permanent. Use it only on collections the user agrees are
  throwaway.
